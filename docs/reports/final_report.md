# BioXplain: an empirical study of explanation stability and cross-cohort replication in breast-tissue gene expression

*Research report. All numbers are taken from saved result files (`docs/results/results_summary.md`, `results/tables/`, `results/sensitivity_summary.json`, `results/preprocessing_sensitivity_summary.json`, run manifests) and are reproducible per `docs/reproducibility.md`. Code commit at freeze: `bed57e4`; this report was written after the external, enrichment and sensitivity runs on top of that freeze.*

## 1. Introduction

**Biological motivation.** Gene-expression profiles separate tumour from normal breast tissue almost perfectly, and machine-learning models are routinely used to nominate "important genes". Whether those genes are reproducible properties of the disease, of the sample composition, or of the analytic choices of the modeller is a separate and less studied question.

**Computational problem.** Expression matrices have tens of thousands of correlated features and, here, only 121 samples (17 normal). In this regime a model's top-ranked genes can change substantially with the training subset, the model class, the explanation method and even a regularisation setting, while predictive accuracy stays at ceiling. Explanation methods measure different quantities (a linear weight, an additive attribution of the model output, a loss increase under permutation) and are known to disagree (Krishna et al. 2022, snippet-level record in `docs/research/literature_update_2026.csv`).

**Research gap (evidence-based, not a novelty claim).** Stability of feature selection is a long-known problem (Ein-Dor et al. 2005; Haury et al. 2011; Nogueira et al. 2018) and stable, leakage-safe selection tools exist (Stabl, OmicSelector, RobustModelMaker; `docs/research/open_source_review.md`). What we did not find in our (limited, mostly abstract-level) search is an empirical study that, on one transcriptomic task, (i) measures chance-corrected stability separately across resampling, model, explainer and their combination with a permutation-label null under an identical pipeline, (ii) includes a simple univariate baseline, and (iii) tests whether internal stability predicts replication in an independent cohort **beyond discovery effect size**, together with an explicit check of tissue-composition confounding. This report is such an empirical evaluation with a tested, reproducible workflow; it does not claim a new algorithm or that stability is a new concept.

**Research question.** How consistently do machine-learning explanations identify gene-expression features across models, explainers and resampling, and does explanation stability predict replication on an independent breast-cancer cohort?

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Datasets
- **Discovery: GSE42568** (NCBI GEO; Affymetrix HG-U133 Plus 2.0, GPL570; GC-RMA): 121 samples, 104 breast cancers and 17 normal breast tissues, 54,675 probes. Labels are reproducible from four concordant metadata fields.
- **External: GSE65194** (GPL570; GC-RMA followed by submitter batch/hybridisation correction): 178 arrays = 130 unique tumours (23 measured twice), 11 healthy tissues, 14 cell lines. Analysis units: 130 tumours (duplicate arrays averaged per probe) + 11 healthy; cell lines excluded. No shared GSM with the discovery cohort. Because both cohorts use the same chip, this is independent-cohort, not independent-technology, validation.
- **Annotation: GPL570.annot (2016).** 42,894 probes map to exactly one gene; 42,892 of them are present in both series matrices and form the feature universe (20,848 genes).
- Reconnaissance findings that shaped the design (`docs/data_reconnaissance.md`): different numeric scales (a hard floor at 2.3128 in 28.5% of discovery values; none externally), class-blocked GEO order, partial confounding of processing date and title prefix with class in discovery, technical duplicates and cell lines externally.

### 2.2 Leakage prevention
Every learned operation (floor-detection expression filter, probe-to-gene collapse by maximum training mean, z-scoring, model fitting, every explainer, the Welch-t baseline) is fitted on the training fold only. The validation fold enters only `transform`/`predict`. Tests: a fit-index spy, an invariance test that corrupts the validation rows and requires every learned artefact of every pipeline to stay bit-identical, a deliberately leaky runner that must be detected, a demonstration of selection leakage on noise labels, and mutation tests on the real code. Splits are shuffled, stratified repeated 5-fold CV (GEO order is class-blocked).

### 2.3 Models (fixed, pre-specified, class-balanced, no tuning)
L2 logistic regression (C = 1), linear SVM (C = 1), random forest (500 trees), XGBoost (300 trees, depth 3, `n_jobs = 4` pinned). Two logistic-regression settings, C = 0.1 and C = 100, are analytic-sensitivity settings and are never primary. Primary metrics are ROC-AUC and normal-class average precision with stratified bootstrap intervals; balanced accuracy, sensitivity and specificity depend on each model's fixed decision threshold.

### 2.4 Explainers (three different quantities; none is causal)
- **Coefficients** (LR, SVM): the fitted linear weight on z-scored genes, conditional on all other genes.
- **SHAP**: mean absolute additive attribution of the model output over all training-fold samples (LinearExplainer for LR/SVM, TreeExplainer for RF/XGBoost); one common explanation population for every model.
- **Permutation importance**: exact (no top-N filter) loss increase under permutation on the training fold, class-balanced Brier loss, R = 10; computed exactly with three shortcuts (analytic for linear models, unused genes have exactly zero importance for trees, per-tree/sparse recomputation for RF/XGBoost) verified against brute force to 1e-12.
- **Welch-t baseline**: |t| on the training fold, ranking only; no p-value is interpreted.

A gene is eligible for a top-k set only if its attribution is strictly positive, so sparse models never receive alphabetical filler genes.

### 2.5 Stability
Sets = top-k genes, k = 10, 25, 50. Nogueira's chance-corrected Φ (JMLR 2018, Definition 4; checked against the paper and stabm reference values, equal to Kuncheva's index for constant size) is the primary measure, with Jaccard and Kuncheva (constant-size sets only) as secondary. Four dimensions are reported separately, never combined: **resampling** (one pipeline across the 5 folds of a repeat), **model** (fixed explainer, different models, within the same fold), **explainer** (fixed model, different explainers, within fold) and **combined** (all ten primary pipelines within fold), plus agreement with the t-test baseline and across the logistic-regression sensitivity settings. The unit of comparison is one CV repeat. Nogueira's analytic confidence interval assumes independent sets and is not valid for overlapping CV training sets; spread across the 20 repeats is reported instead.

### 2.6 Permutation-label null
Each of 30 replicates permutes the class labels globally (class counts preserved) and runs the identical pipeline (same preprocessing, feature universe, 13 pipelines, k values) for one 5-fold repeat, so observed and null statistics are computed by the same function and are matched in resampling number. Results are descriptive (null percentiles versus observed repeats); no p-value is claimed (the smallest attainable exceedance with 30 replicates is 1/31).

### 2.7 Consensus and freeze
No composite score is used. For each gene: selection frequency per pipeline, number of pipelines/models/explainers with frequency ≥ 0.5, median and best rank. Pre-specified consensus rule: frequency ≥ 0.5 at k = 25 in at least 5 of the 10 primary pipelines. Frozen lists: `S_cons`, `S_top25`, `S_top50`. The discovery configuration, code commit, data hashes and frozen gene table are recorded in `docs/freeze/` before the external cohort was opened; the external loader refuses to run otherwise (checked: the freeze commit must be an ancestor of `HEAD` and the frozen gene table unmodified).

### 2.8 External replication (scale-free)
Gene effect = Mann-Whitney AUC − 0.5 in each cohort at the frozen probe (invariant to monotone rescaling). Aligned external effect r_g = sign(discovery effect) × external effect. **C1**: Spearman correlation between internal stability s_g (mean selection frequency, k = 25, primary pipelines; zero for never-selected genes) and r_g across all eligible genes. **C2**: association of s_g with r_g after accounting for the size of the discovery effect |e_disc| (rank regression coefficient and partial Spearman), i.e. whether stability carries information beyond effect size. Uncertainty by stratified bootstrap over external samples (1,000 resamples). Signature level: within-cohort z-scored up-minus-down score with bootstrap AUC; sample level: logistic regression fitted on discovery restricted to `S_top25`, applied after within-cohort standardisation. Sensitivity: replicate-A-only duplicate handling; per-subtype analyses (Luminal, HER2, TNBC vs healthy).

### 2.9 Enrichment and tissue composition
Own hypergeometric over-representation test; background = eligible gene universe ∩ library genes; terms with 5–500 genes; Benjamini-Hochberg within library; libraries GO Biological Process 2023, Reactome 2022, MSigDB Hallmark 2020 (downloaded once, hashed, dated). Tissue-composition hypothesis: adipocyte marker panel (ADIPOQ, PLIN1, FABP4 primary; LEP, LPL, CFD, ADH1B, CIDEC, PLIN4, CD36, GPD1 exploratory), its score in both cohorts, correlation with the frozen signature, residual signature AUC, and a composition-ablation rerun with the panel genes removed from the universe.

### 2.10 Processing-date sensitivity
Dec-2004-only subset (97 tumours + 8 normals, samples processed 2004-12) and a size-matched random control (same class counts), 5 repeats × 4 folds, compared with the full cohort; a date-related change is claimed only if it exceeds the change seen in the size-matched control.

### 2.11 Reproducibility
Seeds, environment, data hashes, resolved model configurations and code commit are stored in every manifest (`docs/reproducibility.md`). Two runs are bit-identical, including across hash seeds and (for XGBoost, after a fix) across fold-parallelism worker counts. 160 tests.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Predictive performance (Table 3, Figure 3)
All seven configurations (four primary models, two LR sensitivity settings, the t-test+LR baseline) classify tumour vs normal near ceiling: pooled out-of-fold ROC-AUC 0.975–0.997, with overlapping 95% bootstrap intervals (logreg 0.997 [0.989, 1.00]; svm 0.995 [0.985, 1.00]; rf 0.995 [0.984, 1.00]; xgb 0.988 [0.971, 0.999]; ttest_lr 0.975 [0.925, 1.00]). Per the pre-specification, **no model is declared better**; this near-ceiling separability is an observation about the cohort, not a success criterion. Balanced accuracy, sensitivity and specificity differ mainly through each model's uncalibrated fixed threshold (e.g. logreg sensitivity 1.00/specificity 0.86 vs svm 0.93/0.99).

### 3.2 Explanation stability (Table 4, Figures 4–5)

**Resampling stability** (one pipeline across the 5 folds of a repeat, mean Nogueira Φ at k = 25 over 20 repeats) ranges from 0.14 (`rf|shap`) to 0.59 (`svm|perm`); only the SVM pipelines and the two off-primary LR settings exceed 0.40 ("intermediate to good" on Nogueira et al.'s own scale); none reaches 0.75 ("excellent"). The primary LR pipeline (`logreg|coef`, C = 1) is markedly less stable (Φ = 0.22) than the same model at C = 0.1 (0.40) or C = 100 (0.42) — the regime-transition finding already established in the regularisation-sensitivity analysis (D11c) is reproduced at full scale (20 repeats): `logreg_coef_across_C` agreement across the three C settings is only 0.29.

**Model and explainer dimensions**, computed strictly within one training fold, are almost uniformly worse than resampling stability: fixing the explainer and comparing across the four models gives Φ = 0.09–0.23; fixing the model and comparing across explainers gives Φ = 0.18 (LR), 0.45 (SVM), 0.75 (RF) and 0.98 (XGBoost). The apparent XGBoost result is a **support-collapse artefact**, not independent agreement: XGBoost's fitted trees use only about 25 genes per fold (Figure 7's `xgb|shap`/`xgb|perm` columns are visibly near-identical), so its two explainers necessarily select almost the same set. **Combined** agreement across all ten primary pipelines is Φ = 0.17 — poor by the same scale. Agreement with the model-free **t-test baseline** (Figure 5B "baseline") is essentially zero for both linear models (coef, perm: Φ ≈ 0.00) and modest for the tree models (Φ = 0.21–0.25), i.e. **trees agree with a simple univariate filter more than the linear models' own coefficients do**.

### 3.3 Observed stability vs the permutation-label null (Table 4d, Figure 6)
Null classification performance (30 replicates) is at chance (mean ROC-AUC 0.47–0.50 per model), confirming the null destroys the discriminative signal as intended. Comparing observed (20 repeats) to null (30 replicates) resampling Φ at k = 25:

- **Clearly above the null:** SVM (coefficients and permutation importance: 100% of observed repeats above the null's 97.5th percentile, and above the null's maximum), XGBoost (95–100%), LR at C = 100 (100%), and the tree-vs-t-test baseline agreement (100%).
- **Not distinguishable from, or below, the null:** the **primary** LR pipeline (`logreg|coef`, `logreg|perm`, `logreg|shap`) is *below* the null median for all three explainers (0% of repeats exceed the null 97.5th percentile); RF is only weakly above the null (55–70%); the t-test baseline itself is weakly distinguishable (35%); model-dimension and explainer-dimension agreement (except XGBoost's support-collapse artefact) never clears the null.
- **A genuinely unexpected pattern:** the LR cross-C agreement is *lower* under real labels (0.30) than under permuted labels (0.70) — i.e., the three regularisation settings agree with each other *more* when the labels carry no signal than when they carry real signal. We interpret this descriptively (not mechanistically): under permuted labels there is no true separating direction, so all three C settings are pulled toward similar noise-fitting solutions, whereas under real labels the two regularisation regimes identified in Section 3.2 pull the fitted direction apart. This is reported as an observation, not explained further.

No p-value is claimed anywhere in this comparison (B = 30 replicates limits the smallest attainable exceedance to 1/31); the descriptive picture above is the finding.

### 3.4 Consensus and the top stable genes (Table 5, Figure 7)
Across 20 repeats × 5 folds × 10 primary pipelines, 1,910 distinct genes appear in at least one top-25 set. The pre-specified consensus rule (own-pipeline selection frequency ≥ 0.5 in at least 5 of 10 primary pipelines) is met by **exactly one gene**, `DKFZp779M0652` (mean selection frequency 0.48). The next most frequent genes (`ABHD1` 0.40, `LYVE1` 0.39, `MMP28` 0.37, `DMGDH` 0.36, ...) each satisfy the frequency-≥0.5 criterion in only 1–4 of the 10 pipelines. Most of the top-30 list are uncharacterised or non-canonical identifiers (LOC*, a DKFZ clone, pseudogenes/antisense transcripts `ZNRF2P1`, `ANKRD10-IT1`); `LYVE1` (a lymphatic endothelial marker) is the one entry with an immediately recognisable, composition-relevant annotation. These are reported as **model-supported, selection-frequency-ranked genes**, not as biomarkers.

### 3.5 Discovery-side sensitivity analyses
- **Preprocessing (D6/D7, 2-repeat comparison against the same repeats of the full run):** replacing the max-mean probe-collapse rule with max-variance, or moving the expression-filter threshold to 0.25× or 1.0× the minority-class size, changes the top-25 list mildly (19–23 of 25 genes retained, Jaccard 0.61–0.85) and the mean |Φ| change at k = 25 is 0.026–00.035; predictive performance is essentially unchanged (ROC-AUC within 0.01 of the reference for every model).
- **Regularisation (D11c, full 20-repeat scale, Section 3.2):** materially changed — confirmed, not merely a 2-repeat artefact.
- **Tissue-composition ablation (removing the 11 panel genes from the universe, 2-repeat comparison):** top-25 overlap with the same repeats of the full run is 21/25 (Jaccard 0.72), universe-wide selection-frequency Spearman 0.64, mean |Φ| change 0.021, performance unchanged. None of the 11 panel genes individually reached the top-25 of the full run (highest frequency: `FABP4` 0.076; `ADIPOQ`, `CFD`, `LEP`, `CIDEC`, `PLIN4`, `GPD1` all 0.0). Removing them barely perturbs the discovery-side stability results.
- **Processing date (D19b, Figure 9):** the Dec-2004-only subset (97 tumours + 8 normals) changes resampling Φ by a mean |Δ| of 0.107 relative to the full cohort, versus 0.065 for a size-matched random control — by the pre-specified magnitude rule, this modestly exceeds the sampling-noise baseline, suggesting a small date-associated component on top of subsampling noise. However, by **gene-identity overlap** the picture is different: the Dec-2004 subset's top-25 list overlaps the full cohort's exactly as much as the random control's does (Jaccard 0.25 in both cases), while the Dec-2004 and control subsets resemble each other slightly more (Jaccard 0.39) than either resembles the full cohort — consistent with sample-size effects dominating over a specific date effect on *which* genes are selected, even though the magnitude of Φ shows a small extra date-associated shift. With only 8 normals in this subset, none of these comparisons should be over-interpreted.

### 3.6 External replication on GSE65194 (Table, Figure 8)
Analysis set: 130 tumours (23 duplicated-array tumours averaged) + 11 healthy = 141 units.

**Gene level** (20,118 eligible genes, all pre-specified, no external tuning): C1 (Spearman between internal selection frequency s_g and the aligned external effect) = **0.119** [95% CI 0.096, 0.136]; restricted to genes with s_g > 0, C1 = 0.241 [0.210, 0.256]. C2 (partial Spearman of s_g with the aligned external effect, controlling for |discovery effect|) = **0.080** [0.065, 0.087] — small but its interval excludes zero, i.e. **internal stability carries a small amount of external-replication information beyond discovery effect size alone**. Direction consistency across all 20,118 genes is 60.3% [59.6%, 61.0%] — modestly, but not dramatically, above the 50% chance level (this interval is descriptive: genes are correlated, so it should not be read as an inferential test). Results are essentially unchanged using replicate-A-only duplicate handling (C1 = 0.120) and within every subtype-vs-healthy comparison separately (Luminal 0.127, HER2 0.114, TNBC 0.097; direction consistency 59–60% throughout).

**List and sample level:** the frozen `S_top25` and `S_top50` lists show much stronger apparent replication — 92% and 94% direction consistency, external signature AUC 0.995 and 1.00, and a discovery-frozen logistic-regression classifier restricted to `S_top25` achieves external ROC-AUC 0.999 (balanced accuracy 0.905, sensitivity 0.99, specificity 0.82 at 9/11 healthy correctly called). `S_cons` has only one gene and is not evaluable at the list level.

**Tissue-composition check (this is the central qualifying result for Section 3.6):** independently of the frozen signature, all 11 adipose-marker panel genes individually show AUC ≈ 0.00–0.04 (i.e. far higher expression in normal than in cancer tissue) in **both** cohorts (discovery AUC 0.055–0.115; external AUC 0.000–0.037), which is itself biologically expected (normal breast tissue is adipocyte-rich) and internally consistent evidence that the panel behaves as intended. The frozen `S_top25` signature score correlates strongly and negatively with this composition-panel score externally (r = −0.80 overall; −0.31 within cancer, −0.73 within normal). After linearly regressing the composition-panel score out of the signature score, the **external** signature AUC collapses from 0.995 to **0.57** (near chance), whereas the same operation on the **discovery** (in-sample) fit barely changes it (1.00 → 0.95). None of the 11 panel genes individually reached `S_top25` or `S_cons`. We read this as: the frozen signature's strong external replication is **substantially attributable to an adipose/tissue-composition-associated axis** that the signature's genes track collectively (as a proxy), even though the signature is not composed of canonical adipose-marker genes by name — a plausible interpretation, not a proven mechanism (no cell-type deconvolution was performed).

### 3.7 Biological enrichment (Table, `results/enrichment/`)
Foreground = `S_top50` (34/21/13 of its 50 genes present in the GO BP / Reactome / Hallmark 2023–2020 gene universes respectively; `S_cons` had too few genes, correctly skipped per the pre-specified 10-gene minimum). **No term reaches Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p < 0.05 in any of the three libraries** (minimum adjusted p 0.51–1.00). This is an honest null result and is not treated as evidence of absence, only as an absence of the specific over-representation signal this test can detect at this foreground size.

## 4. Discussion

**What the results show.** (1) Predictive performance is near ceiling for every model and is uninformative for distinguishing methodological choices here, exactly as anticipated. (2) Explanation stability is genuinely low for most model–explainer combinations and — critically — depends strongly on *analytic choices that have no effect on predictive performance*: which model, which explainer, and for logistic regression, which regularisation strength. The permutation null shows that only a subset of pipelines (SVM's two explainers, XGBoost, LR away from its primary C) are clearly more stable than chance; the primary, pre-specified logistic-regression configuration is *not* distinguishable from — and on 20/20 repeats numerically below — its own null. (3) A composite frozen signature built from the most internally stable genes replicates strikingly well in an independent cohort at the signature and sample level, but a gene-level, less aggregated analysis shows this replication is modest (C1 ≈ 0.12) and that a large share of the signature-level replication is attributable to a tissue-composition-associated signal rather than confirmed as tumour-specific biology. (4) Enrichment of the most consistently selected genes shows no significant pathway signal at this scale.

**What the results do not show.** They do not show that any specific gene is a disease-causing gene, a validated biomarker, or clinically actionable. They do not show that one model or explainer is "correct" — the disagreement between them is the finding, not a defect to be resolved. They do not establish that internal stability is a reliable proxy for external validity in general; here it is a small, statistically distinguishable but practically modest predictor at the gene level, while appearing to be a much stronger predictor at the aggregated signature level for reasons that our composition analysis suggests are partly non-specific.

**Relation to prior work.** The core disagreement-between-explainers observation matches Krishna et al.'s (2022) general finding for tabular data, extended here to a high-dimensional, small-n transcriptomic setting with a formal chance correction and a permutation null, neither of which appears standard in that literature (`docs/research/literature_update_2026.csv`, L18–L21, all at snippet/abstract verification level). Ein-Dor et al. (2005) and Haury et al. (2011) established gene-set instability across studies and selection methods; this study adds a within-one-cohort, within-one-pipeline decomposition (resampling vs model vs explainer) and a null-referenced answer to "how stable relative to chance". The regularisation-strength result — that predictive performance is unaffected while the *identity* of top genes depends strongly on hyperparameter choice, with the model's default/primary setting being the least stable of five tested values — is, as far as our (non-exhaustive) search found, not previously reported in this form.

**Limitations.** See `docs/limitations.md` for the full list (data, methods, and interpretation limits established before any result was seen); the results-specific additions are: (a) the LR-cross-C "more agreement under the null than under real labels" pattern is unexplained and should be treated as an observation requiring further investigation, not a mechanism; (b) the processing-date sensitivity gives mixed signals (a modest magnitude effect, no excess effect on gene identity) and cannot fully resolve the known date/class confounding with only 8 normals in the reduced subset; (c) the composition analysis uses linear regression of one score on another and a fixed panel of 11 genes chosen from general adipocyte biology, not a validated deconvolution method; (d) enrichment was tested only at the pre-specified `S_top50` foreground size, so weaker but real pathway signals at other list sizes are not excluded by this null result; (e) both cohorts share the GPL570 platform, so external replication here is independent-cohort, not independent-technology, validation.

**Implications.** For anyone building a gene-expression classifier and reporting "important genes": (i) predictive performance alone does not certify the genes are anything more than one analytic path's output; (ii) a stability check against a permutation null, decomposed by resampling/model/explainer, is cheap relative to the modelling itself and can reveal that a pipeline's default configuration is markedly *less* reproducible than nearby alternatives; (iii) strong signature-level external replication should be checked against plausible non-specific confounders (here, tissue composition) before being read as evidence of disease-specific biology.

## 6. Conclusion
This study finds that, in a high-dimensional, small-normal-group breast-tissue classification task, near-ceiling predictive performance coexists with explanation stability that is low for most model–explainer pipelines and strongly dependent on model, explainer and (for logistic regression) regularisation choices that do not affect predictive accuracy. Against a matched permutation-label null, only a subset of pipelines show stability clearly distinguishable from chance. Internal selection-frequency stability shows a small, bootstrap-distinguishable association with independent-cohort gene-level replication that persists beyond discovery effect size (C2 partial Spearman 0.08), while a composite signature built from the most stable genes replicates strongly at the signature level — an effect our tissue-composition analysis suggests is substantially, though not exclusively, attributable to a composition-associated signal rather than confirmed tumour-specific biology. No enrichment signal was detected. These findings are reported as an empirical evaluation and a reproducible, tested workflow (`docs/reproducibility.md`), not as the discovery of causal genes, validated biomarkers, or a novel algorithm.
